S6. Model specification and priors

Primary model (M1), for study \(s\), arm \(i\):

\[ \text{events}_i \mid n_i \sim \text{Binomial}\!\left(n_i,\ \text{logit}^{-1}\!\left(\alpha + \beta_{\text{PTCy}} x_i + \beta_{\text{tp}} t_i + u_{s[i]}\right)\right), \qquad u_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2) \]

where \(x_i\) indicates a PTCy arm and \(t_i\) indicates a late (≥1 year) rather than early (day +100 or +180) timepoint.

The steroid-mediation model (M2) adds arm-level systemic steroid exposure percentage, mean-centred, as an additional fixed effect.

MCMC: four chains × 4000 iterations (1000 warmup), adapt_delta = 0·95, giving 12 000 post-warmup draws per model.

S7. MCMC diagnostics

All Bayesian models were fitted with brms v2.23.0 and the rstan backend. R̂ (threshold < 1·01), bulk and tail effective sample size (ESS; threshold > 400), and the number of divergent transitions are reported for every fitted model. Values are the worst case (maximum R̂, minimum ESS) across all model parameters.

All models except C2 BK achieved R̂ < 1·01 with zero divergent transitions and bulk ESS above 1000. The C2 BK model (k = 3) did not converge to that standard (R̂ = 1·0592, bulk ESS 67, tail ESS 17) and its posterior should be read as indicative only.

All models show adequate calibration, with points clustered near the identity line and symmetric scatter. The C1 IFI model (k = 6) shows the widest scatter, consistent with its large τ posterior ((fig-s7c?)) and the heterogeneity identified in S9. No systematic over- or under-prediction is evident in any model.

S8. Forest plots

Individual study odds ratios with 95% CIs and the pooled frequentist random-effects estimate (metafor, REML), shown alongside the Bayesian estimates reported in the main text as concordance checks. All plots are from the cohort-deduplicated datasets.

S9. Sensitivity analyses

Full frequentist results for all models are provided in freq_results_setB.csv, a supplementary data file, which carries the deduplicated fits where deduplication applied and the original fits otherwise (see its source column). Direction was concordant between Bayesian and frequentist estimates for every outcome. Two framework-specific differences are worth noting: C1 BSI (Bayesian odds ratio 1·87 versus frequentist 1·29 [0·61–2·73]) and C1 IFI (0·43 versus 0·60 [0·21–1·74]), both reflecting the influence of informative priors at small k.

S10. Publication bias

Robust Bayesian model-averaging (RoBMA) models were fitted for all outcomes with k ≥ 6.

S13. PROSPERO registration

The study protocol was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261372156) before data extraction. Protocol amendments were: (1) a Bayesian rather than frequentist primary analysis framework; (2) addition of BK virus reactivation, infection-related mortality, and relapse-related mortality as secondary outcomes; and (3) addition of the steroid-mediation (M2) model.
