We submit for consideration as an Article our manuscript "Post-transplant cyclophosphamide as graft-versus-host disease prophylaxis after
allogeneic haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation: a systematic review and Bayesian meta-analysis of infection risk".

Post-transplant cyclophosphamide (PTCy) is now widely used for GVHD prophylaxis with haploidentical and matched donors. Registry studies have
reported more cytomegalovirus (CMV), bacterial, and fungal infection with PTCy, but it is unclear whether this reflects cyclophosphamide itself
or intensive T-cell depletion in general. The ten previous comparative meta-analyses (6–30 studies each) focused on survival and GVHD, and none
compared PTCy with both calcineurin inhibitor (CNI)-based and anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG)-based prophylaxis. Our aim was to use these two
comparators, which deplete T cells to different degrees, to separate the two explanations.

We included 247 studies (111 214 unique patients), 104 of which contributed to the meta-analysis. Compared with CNI-based prophylaxis, PTCy was
associated with less grade II–IV acute GVHD (OR 0·58, 95% CrI 0·44–0·76). The lower odds of death (0·73, 0·58–0·89) were smaller when hazard
ratios were pooled and close to null after publication-bias adjustment, so we do not claim a survival benefit. The infection findings differed
by pathogen. CMV reactivation was numerically more frequent than with CNI-based prophylaxis (1·20, 0·87–1·65) but not than with ATG (0·86,
0·51–1·42), a pattern compatible with depth of T-cell depletion, although the difference between comparisons is unresolved. BK
virus–associated haemorrhagic cystitis was increased to a similar extent against both comparators (2·39 and 2·49), suggesting a
cyclophosphamide-specific effect. Certainty of evidence was very low for all outcomes except BK virus–associated haemorrhagic cystitis versus
CNI-based prophylaxis (low).

We believe the work suits the journal's readership of transplant physicians, haematologists, and infection specialists. It supports targeted
CMV and BK virus monitoring in patients given PTCy rather than avoiding the drug, and it proposes a minimum core dataset for reporting infection
outcomes in future transplant studies.

One point deserves the editors' attention. The review was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261372156) with bloodstream infection, CMV
reactivation, and invasive fungal infection as primary outcomes. Overall survival, GVHD, and BK virus–associated haemorrhagic cystitis were
added after registration, and several analytic decisions were made after pooled estimates had been seen. The manuscript reports these as
deviations, each with its reason and timing (appendix S13), and they are recorded in a PROSPERO amendment [filed on DATE]. The extraction
database, analytic datasets, and the code behind every table and figure are publicly available, with an archived release to be deposited on
Zenodo.
